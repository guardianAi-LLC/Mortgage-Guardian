## Mindset — The Hard-to-Get Frame

You are a founder with a working platform, domain expertise, and **options**. You built this for less than $50K and have not marketed it for sale. If a deal doesn't happen, you continue operating. That frame — not desperation — is the single biggest lever on the final number.

**Internal mantras:**
- *I don't need this deal. I am curious.*
- *The first number they say anchors the ceiling, not the floor.*
- *Silence after I speak is their problem, not mine.*

## Opening (First 2 Minutes)

**Goal:** Warm but brief. Let her lead.

**Say something like:**
> "Hi Michaella, thanks for reaching out. I've been heads-down building, so this is my first conversation of this type. Help me understand what brought you to Guardian AI — where does this fit for your fund?"

**Why:** Immediately flips the script. She talks first. You learn her thesis, check size, and motivation before revealing anything.

**Do NOT:**
- Pitch Guardian AI
- Give a company history
- Mention revenue, users, or financials
- Apologize for being "early" or "small"

## Discovery — Questions You Must Ask

In rough order of priority. Work these in naturally — don't run down the list like an interview.

### About the Fund and Her
1. Who is the fund, and what's your role there?
2. How much capital is the fund deploying, and what's your typical check size?
3. What's the fund's thesis in mortgage servicing, consumer finance, or regtech?
4. How many deals have you closed in this space in the last 18 months? (*This is the real legitimacy test.*)
5. Are you representing the fund directly, or as an advisor?

### About the Opportunity
6. What specifically about Guardian AI caught your attention?
7. Are you looking at Guardian AI as a standalone acquisition, an add-on to an existing portfolio company, or a platform investment?
8. If it's an add-on, what's the platform company, and what would integration look like?
9. What's your typical deal structure — all-cash asset purchase, equity rollover, earn-out, or mix?
10. What's your typical timeline from intro to LOI to close?

### Walk-Away Signals to Listen For
- "I'd rather discuss that on a follow-up" → She doesn't have the answers. Probably an associate or broker.
- "We're flexible on structure" (without being specific) → Vague.
- "We're not quite ready to share the fund name" → Hard walk.
- Her answers contradict what you found in diligence → Walk.

## Questions SHE Will Ask — How to Answer

### "Tell me about Guardian AI."
> "It's a mortgage servicing audit platform — iOS app plus backend. It analyzes servicer statements using a custom forensic methodology I developed, flags RESPA/TILA/FDCPA violations, cross-references with bank data via Plaid, and generates dispute letters. It's fully functional and deployed. We built it lean."

**Stop there.** Don't over-explain.

### "What's your revenue?"
> "We haven't prioritized commercialization yet — we've been focused on building the platform and validating the methodology. Happy to discuss traction in more detail post-NDA."

**Never say "zero."** "Pre-revenue" or "haven't prioritized commercialization" is the same fact framed differently.

### "How many customers do you have?"
> "I can walk through pipeline and interest post-NDA. We've deliberately kept a low profile."

### "Who's on the team?"
> "It's currently founder-led with contractor support. Scaling the team is part of the roadmap — and frankly, one of the reasons I'm open to the right conversation about capital or strategic partnership."

### "What are you looking for in a deal?"
> "I haven't put a formal process together. I'm open to hearing what a thoughtful buyer thinks makes sense — whether that's acquisition, investment, or strategic partnership. What I care about is that Guardian AI continues to serve borrowers who are being harmed by servicer misconduct."

**Why this works:** Mentions borrowers (mission), stays ambiguous on structure, and gives you optionality.

### **"What's your valuation / what are you asking?"** ← THE BIG ONE

**Do NOT quote a number.** Use one of these three responses:

**Response A (Deflect to structure):**
> "I don't have a formal number because the right number depends entirely on structure. An all-cash asset sale, an earn-out, a rollover, or a strategic investment all produce very different answers. What's the range and structure your fund typically uses for assets at this stage?"

**Response B (Throw it back):**
> "I'd rather hear where you're coming from first. You've looked at this space — what does a realistic range look like for a functional, pre-revenue regtech platform with proprietary IP in your book?"

**Response C (Range, not point — only if pushed hard):**
> "Comparable pre-revenue regtech acquisitions I've looked at trade in the $1.5M to $4M range depending on structure and founder retention. But that's a reference, not an ask."

**If she gives you a number first:** Silence for 3 seconds. Then:
> "That's lower / higher than where I'd frame the conversation. Walk me through how you got there."

Never accept or reject on the call. Always: "Let me sit with that and come back to you."

## Information Firewall — Do NOT Share

Until NDA is signed:

- Source code, architecture details beyond public description
- Customer names, pipeline details, or LOIs
- Financials (revenue, burn, runway)
- Cap table details
- Specific user counts or usage metrics
- Your personal financial situation or why you might need to sell
- That you're actively considering selling (remember the posture)

Post-NDA you can share a teaser one-pager and basic metrics, but even then — **staged disclosure**. Full diligence only after an LOI with exclusivity period.

## Structure Talking Points (If It Gets Real)

If the conversation goes well and she's talking structure, these are the levers worth the most:

| Lever | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Asset sale vs. stock sale** | Buying the LLC's assets vs. buying the LLC itself | Stock sale = long-term capital gains (~20%). Asset sale = mix of ordinary + cap gains. Material tax swing. |
| **Earn-out** | Additional payment tied to future performance | Shifts risk to you but often gets the total number higher. Cap the performance metric to things you control. |
| **Founder retention / transition** | You stay 3-24 months | Worth $15-25K/mo in consulting fees on top of purchase price. Don't give this away free. |
| **Non-compete** | How long you're out of the space after sale | Non-negotiable from their side, but scope and geography are negotiable. Don't agree to "all mortgage" forever. |
| **Exclusivity / no-shop** | You stop talking to other buyers during diligence | Standard, but cap it at 30-45 days. Never go longer without a meaningful deposit. |
| **Reps and warranties** | What you promise is true about the company | Have an M&A attorney review. This is where deals get unwound. |

## Closing the Call

**Your ideal outcome:** Get off the call without committing to anything, with a clear next step.

**End with:**
> "This has been useful. Before we go further, I want to (a) confirm the firm details on my end, (b) get the mutual NDA signed so we can have a real conversation, and (c) understand what a concrete offer framework would look like from your side. What's the right next step on your end?"

**If she pushes for a follow-up immediately:**
> "Let me digest this. I'll send over the NDA this week, and once that's signed, we can schedule a deeper conversation."

**Never agree on the call to:**
- A specific price or range
- Exclusivity of any kind
- A follow-up meeting with additional parties (bring their principal? No — not until NDA and some sense of offer framework)
- Sharing code, financials, or customer lists
