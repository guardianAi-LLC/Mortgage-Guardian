# Trap Questions & Scripted Deflections — Friday Call Reference Card

**How to use:** Print this. Have it open in front of you during the call. When Michaella asks one of these, the scripted response is already written — you just need to deliver it calmly.

**Master rule:** Your answer to every operational/financial question before NDA is some variant of *"Post-NDA, with a deal framework in front of us."* Repeat as many times as needed. It is professional, not rude.

---

## The 15 Trap Questions

### 1. "What's your revenue / ARR?"
**Trap:** Assumes paying customers exist. Any specific number anchors your valuation.
✗ *"We don't have revenue yet."* — Kills your leverage instantly.
✓ **Say:** *"We're pre-commercial by design — the platform's built, the methodology's validated, we haven't opened the front door. Commercial ramp is part of the conversation I'd want to have post-NDA with a real deal framework."*
**If pushed:** *"I'd rather talk about what your fund's underwriting model looks like for an asset at this stage than give you a snapshot number out of context."*

---

### 2. "What's your monthly churn?"
**Trap:** Assumes recurring customers. Forces you to expose pre-revenue status.
✓ **Say:** *"Churn economics aren't the right lens yet — that's a post-launch conversation. Happy to walk through the retention model post-NDA."*

---

### 3. "How many customers / users do you have?"
**Trap:** Same as #1. Any small number ("12") becomes the anchor.
✓ **Say:** *"We've kept usage tightly controlled during the build phase. Pipeline and intent are part of the post-NDA discussion."*
**If pushed:** *"I'd rather not quote soft numbers before we have a real framework. What would be more useful — TAM, unit economics, or platform capabilities?"*

---

### 4. "What's your burn rate? How much runway do you have?"
**Trap:** Signals urgency. If she smells cash pressure, your price drops 50%.
✗ *"I've self-funded, so burn is low."* — Accurate but screams "I need this."
✓ **Say:** *"The platform was built capital-efficiently. Operating runway isn't a constraint in how I'm thinking about this conversation."*
**Never** disclose: personal savings, credit position, loan obligations, or that this is your primary income source.

---

### 5. "Why are you open to selling now?"
**Trap:** Classic. Any answer that sounds like "I need to" drops your price. Any answer that sounds like "I don't want to" may end the call.
✓ **Say:** *"I'm not actively running a sale process. I'm open to the right conversation because scaling Guardian AI to its potential requires capital and distribution I can accelerate faster with a strategic partner than alone. The question isn't whether I sell — it's whether the structure makes sense for the mission."*

---

### 6. "What's your asking price / valuation?"
**Trap:** First number anchors the ceiling.
✓ **Say:** *"I don't have a formal number because the right number depends entirely on structure. What's the range and structure your fund typically uses for assets at this stage?"*
**If she gives a number:** Silence 3 seconds. Then: *"That's lower than where I'd frame the conversation. Walk me through how you got there."*
**Never** accept or reject live. Always: *"Let me sit with that."*

---

### 7. "Are you talking to other buyers?"
**Trap:** "Yes" + no specifics = she assumes you're bluffing. "No" = she knows you have no leverage.
✓ **Say:** *"I've had inbound interest I'm evaluating. I'm not running a formal process, but I'm not operating in a vacuum either."*
**Don't name names.** Don't quantify. Ambiguity is leverage.

---

### 8. "Who else is on the team?"
**Trap:** "Just me" = acqui-hire at depressed founder rates. Naming contractors without contracts = IP risk disclosure.
✓ **Say:** *"It's founder-led with specialized contractor support on the build. Team scaling is part of the forward plan — one of the reasons the right partnership is interesting."*

---

### 9. "Do you own all the IP outright? Any contractor IP issues?"
**Trap:** Diligence question disguised as a casual ask. Wrong answer kills the deal later.
✓ **Say:** *"All IP and code ownership is clean and sits with Guardian AI LLC. I'll have the full chain-of-title documentation ready for diligence post-LOI."*
**Before Friday:** Actually verify this. If any contractor doesn't have a signed IP assignment, fix it before diligence. This is the single most common deal-killer at close.

---

### 10. "Any lawsuits, regulatory complaints, or CFPB matters?"
**Trap:** Yes/no question with massive implications. Hedging looks evasive.
✓ **Say:** *"No pending litigation or regulatory action against Guardian AI. The platform operates in a highly regulated space (RESPA, TILA, FDCPA), and we've built compliance into the methodology from day one. Full documentation available during diligence."*

---

### 11. "What's your tech stack? What happens if Anthropic changes pricing?"
**Trap:** Testing your technical depth AND your dependency risk.
✓ **Say:** *"Node/Express backend, Next.js 15 frontend, Swift/SwiftUI iOS. Claude for analysis, Plaid for bank data, Supabase for database. The architecture is model-agnostic — we can swap LLM providers without re-architecting. Dependency risk is manageable and documented."*
**Shows:** Technical fluency + risk awareness. Answer firmly, don't ramble.

---

### 12. "Who are your competitors?"
**Trap:** Name strong competitors = commoditizes you. Say "no one" = you look naive.
✓ **Say:** *"There are adjacent players in mortgage auditing and consumer finance compliance, but nobody combining forensic audit methodology with consumer-grade UX and bank data integration the way Guardian AI does. Happy to walk through the competitive landscape post-NDA."*

---

### 13. "What's your minimum acceptable price?"
**Trap:** Whatever you say becomes their maximum offer. Full stop.
✓ **Say:** *"I don't operate with a minimum because I'm not in a forced-sale posture. What matters is structure, strategic fit, and whether the mission continues. Numbers come after framework."*
**Never** answer this question with a number. Ever.

---

### 14. "Would you stay on post-acquisition?"
**Trap:** Sounds collaborative. Actually pricing your retention. If you seem eager, they pay you consulting rates instead of purchase price.
✓ **Say:** *"I'm open to a transition role if it serves the platform. Scope, duration, and terms would be part of the deal structure — not a free add-on."*
**Frame founder time as a priced line item**, not a favor. $15–25K/mo consulting is standard and should be on top of purchase price.

---

### 15. "Can you send me the financials / code / customer list after the call?"
**Trap:** Classic post-call data grab. No NDA, no gift.
✓ **Say:** *"Happy to share substantive detail once the mutual NDA is executed. I'll send it over after our call, and we can schedule the next conversation once it's signed."*
**Never** send anything proprietary without NDA. If she objects to an NDA, the deal isn't real.

---

## Universal Deflection Phrases (Memorize These)

Use these as bridges when you need a beat to think:

- *"That's a post-NDA conversation, but let me frame it this way..."*
- *"I'd rather understand your framework first before getting into specifics."*
- *"Good question — let me come back to that once we have a deal structure in view."*
- *"What would be more useful for you — [topic A] or [topic B]?"*
- *"Let me sit with that and come back to you."*

---

## What to Do When You Don't Know an Answer

**Never guess. Never wing it.**

✓ **Say:** *"I want to give you an accurate answer, not a quick one. Let me follow up on that after the call."*

Every guess on this call becomes a rep-and-warranty problem at close. "I think it's about..." is how founders get sued post-acquisition.

---

## Red-Flag Responses from HER (Walk Away Signals)

- Refuses to sign an NDA → **Walk.**
- Won't name the fund → **Walk.**
- Pushes hard for financials on Call #1 → **Slow down.**
- Implies an LOI can happen without diligence → **Not a real buyer.**
- Mentions a "success fee" or "engagement fee" from your side → **Broker, not buyer. Walk.**
- Offers to "help you prepare the company for sale" → **Wrong side of the table. Walk.**

---

## Your Three-Part Mental Model for Every Question

1. **Why are they asking this?** (Diligence? Anchor? Trap?)
2. **What does a strong answer preserve?** (Optionality, posture, leverage.)
3. **What's the shortest response that does that?** (Fewer words = more control.)
